# Special needs

Read this whenever `prep/profile.md` lists a child, a senior, a disability, a chronic condition, a medical device, or a pet. These change tiers and quantities; they are not footnotes.

## Children

- Infants and toddlers get their own food and water line (formula, water to prepare it, pouches, snacks), nappies and wipes for the goal period, a comfort item in the go-bag, and their own medication.
- Each child's school or daycare pickup policy goes on the comms card. Who picks up if the usual parent can't?
- Children old enough to carry a card carry one. They learn the out-of-area number and the nearest meeting place.
- Talking about it: calm, honest, short, age-appropriate. "We're ready, we have a plan, we'll look after you." Don't force the conversation; answer questions. The BBK brochure's section on talking with children is the reference; the Nummer gegen Kummer (Germany) and the paediatrician are the help lines if a child is anxious.

## Seniors

- Mobility: can they leave the building quickly without help? If not, a named helper and a route go into red.
- Medication reserve and a written medication plan in the go-bag.
- Spares and batteries for glasses, hearing aids, walking aids.
- A neighbour or relative who checks on them; put it on the card.

## Disability and chronic illness

From ready.gov/disability and the BBK section on Menschen mit Beeinträchtigungen:

- Aids (glasses, hearing aid, wheelchair, communication device) plus spare batteries, chargers and a charged power bank, in the go-bag or next to it.
- Any device that needs mains power (CPAP, oxygen concentrator, ventilator, powered wheelchair, fridge for insulin) is listed with its wattage. That list sizes the power item, which moves from green to amber when a device is life-critical. Tell the grid operator and the local authority about life-critical equipment where they keep such registers.
- Medication plan and a 10-day reserve; discuss with the doctor and pharmacy how to hold a reserve.
- An emergency ID (Notfallausweis) with condition, medication, aids and contacts, and the phone's medical ID filled in. The Notfallausweis also records how the person communicates (if sign language, writing, a device), so responders know.
- Communication with responders: paper and pen in the go-bag; the nora app (Germany) places an emergency call without speaking.
- Smoke alarms with light and vibration for people with hearing loss.
- Escape routes checked for the aid the person uses; if a route is impossible, a helper and an alternative are named.
- Support network: tell neighbours, family, friends how they can help. Put names on the card.

## Pets and assistance animals

Food, water, medication, carrier, lead, vaccination record, a photo, litter. Decide where the animal goes if you evacuate; many shelters don't take pets, so name a friend or a pet-friendly option. An assistance animal goes with its person everywhere; its supplies are in red with the person's.

## Pregnancy and postpartum

Medical records copied, birth plan and hospital on the card, supplies for the baby planned before the due date.

## Language

If anyone in the household doesn't read the local language well, the card is bilingual, and the alert app is set to a language they read.
